# Chapter 14. B5F

The position is a staircase.

That's what the radio calls it, the last time the radio says anything with all its words in the right order. *North teams, hold the north entrances. Keep civilians moving out. Do not go further in.* Ijichi's voice, or someone doing a very good impression of Ijichi's voice. Calm, in that particular way.

So they hold a staircase.

It's on the Meiji-dori side, north of the station, where a covered stairway goes down from the pavement into the underground concourse. Grey tiles. A steel handrail down the middle, polished by a million hands that weren't thinking about it. A sign with a little train on it and an arrow pointing down, and under the arrow, in smaller letters, the names of the lines. Fukutoshin is the last one.

Down is the station. Up is the street. They stand at the top, between the two.

*Promoted,* he thinks. *From railing to staircase.*

---

For a while it's people.

They come up out of the concourse in clumps of five and ten, blinking, as if they've been in a cinema and the film went wrong. Some are still in costume. Most of the costumes have stopped being funny. A man dressed as a mummy comes up trailing a long loose bandage from his ankle and keeps treading on it, and doesn't seem to notice, and keeps going. A boy carrying a girl on his back, both of them wearing the same rabbit ears. A woman in a business suit with a pumpkin painted on one cheek, only one, as if somebody had started on her and been interrupted.

"Up the street," Kusakabe says. "Left at the corner. Keep going."

They go. Nobody asks him anything. He likes them for it.

Panda walks the slow ones to the corner and comes back. Walks some more. Comes back. He's quick about it and patient at the same time, which is a combination Kusakabe has only ever seen in Panda and a few nurses. People flinch when they first see him. Then they take his paw. It's a very big paw and there's something about it that makes people hold on.

Between clumps, it's quiet. Not real quiet. There's the hum under the ground that he can feel through his shoes, and every so often something heavier, far off, like furniture being moved in a flat three floors down. Smoke somewhere. Panda's radio, clipped to his collar, coughing up pieces of sentences.

"—Dogenzaka, can anyone—"

"—confirm, the users are on the—"

"—no, the other—"

He doesn't try to make it into anything. You can't make one sentence out of the ends of twenty.

His forearm has stiffened where the coat sleeve stuck to the cut. He bends it now and then to keep it honest.

Panda's shoulder is still dark where the fur spiked in the escalator fight. Some of it might be the curse's. Some of it might be Panda's. Kusakabe has never asked what Panda has where other people have blood, and he isn't going to start tonight. Panda doesn't seem to notice it. He keeps touching the radio on his collar instead, as if it's a sore tooth.

---

The first thing to come up the stairs that isn't a person comes up at nineteen minutes to nine.

He feels it before it turns the landing. A touch at the edge of the circle, light and cold, like someone laying the back of a finger on his wrist.

Then it's in sight. It comes up the steps on too many legs, pale and quick, and it's wearing faces. That's the first thing he sees and the only thing he'll remember. Rubber faces, the cheap kind from the discount shops, witches and skulls and grinning old men, stacked one over another all down the length of it. They slide as it moves. Under each face is another face. Under that one, nothing he wants to know about.

*Halloween,* he thinks. *Even the curses dressed up.*

It comes at the top step where he's standing because he's what's standing there.

The line breaks. His hands answer. One cut across, low, where the legs meet the rest. The weight of it coming on anyway, the way a falling thing keeps falling after it's dead. He turns his shoulder and lets it go past him, and it goes over the handrail and down into the stairwell in two pieces, faces coming off it as it goes, slapping on the tiles.

Then the grey. Then the smell of hot rubber.

A witch's face lies on the top step by his foot, grinning up at him with nothing behind it.

He nudges it down the stairs with his toe.

His heart is going. It always goes. He waits for it the way you wait for a kettle to stop rattling after you've turned it off.

"That's three tonight," Panda says, coming back from the corner. "Of the big ones. I'm counting."

"Don't."

"Okay." A pause. "It's three, though."

---

Tanabe comes up the stairs at ten to nine.

Tanabe, or Tanaka. Ten months since Shinjuku, a dozen times passing in corridors, and he still doesn't know which. It was too late to ask the first time. It's much too late now.

Tanabe has someone over his shoulder. A young auxiliary, a woman in a black suit with one shoe, her arm round his neck, her head down. Tanabe's left eye is swollen shut. There's blood dried in his eyebrow like rust on a gate. He gets to the top step and stops there, breathing, and looks at Kusakabe with the eye that still works.

"Kusakabe."

"Tanabe."

He doesn't correct it. So that's something, or it's nothing. Either way it's settled.

"Where's the medical point?"

"Up. Corner. Left. Someone'll have a van."

Tanabe nods. He shifts the auxiliary on his shoulder. She makes a small noise and goes quiet again.

Panda is fifty metres up the street, with a mother and two small boys in matching dinosaur suits, and has his back to them.

"Don't go past the concourse," Tanabe says.

"Wasn't going to."

"I mean it." He wipes his good eye with the back of his wrist. "B5F. The Fukutoshin platform. Five floors down. Right at the bottom of all of it." He swallows. "There's special grades down there. Not one. More than one. Gojo's down there with them."

The word *grades*, with the *s* on the end, goes into Kusakabe like a cold key dropped down the back of a collar.

"Who else?"

"Nobody, that I know of. Some went down early, before it all closed up. Nobody's come back up from that level to say anything." Tanabe looks at the stairwell behind him, at the arrow on the sign. "The radio was talking about it. Then it wasn't. That's all I've got."

"Okay."

"I'm not going back down."

"No."

"I've got her." Tanabe moves the woman's arm a little higher on his neck, as if she's the reason, and maybe she is. "Hold your stairs."

"Yeah."

He goes. Up the street, past Panda, towards the corner. He doesn't look back. Kusakabe watches him go and thinks about calling something after him, his name, the right one, and doesn't.

---

Panda comes back without the dinosaurs.

The radio on his collar wakes up as he comes. A woman's voice this time, high and too fast, half of it gone to static.

"—repeat, B5F—special grade—Gojo-san is—platform level—anyone who can—"

Then nothing.

Panda stops on the top step. He's very still. He looks down at the radio on his own collar, which is difficult for him, because of the shape of his head, so he looks at it sideways, the way a dog looks at a noise.

"Sensei. Did you hear that?"

"Yeah."

"B5F."

"Yeah."

"That's where Satoru is."

"Yeah."

"Special grades," Panda says. "She said special grades." He looks at the stairwell. The arrow. The word *Fukutoshin* at the bottom of the list. "That's this line. That's straight down from here."

Kusakabe says nothing.

"We should go down," Panda says.

He says it simply. Not bravely. That's the worst of it. He says it the way he'd say it's raining, or the way he'd hand someone a sweet. There are people down there. Satoru is down there. The stairs are right here. So.

And Kusakabe feels it.

A cold sliding feeling in his stomach. Something that was sitting on a shelf inside him, heavy, minding its own business, and has started to slide off the edge. Slowly, and then not slowly.

*Special grades.* Plural. Things that don't care about a circle. Things that don't care about a sword, or how long you practised, or how good your feet are. Things that have looked at men like him for longer than there have been men like him, and been bored.

Five floors down. In the dark. With whatever built the curtain.

He thinks, for no reason at all, of his sister's window lock. Whether he checked it on Sunday. He did. Twice.

And then he hears his own voice.

"I heard they've already got people down there."

It comes out easy. That's what he'll remember afterwards. How easy. Not a stammer, not a pause. Level and tired and a little bored, his ordinary voice, the one he uses for *Traffic*. As if the lie had been sitting ready in his mouth all night, already shaped, waiting for the door to open.

Panda turns his head and looks at him.

It's a long look. Panda's face doesn't do much. It isn't built to. There's no eyebrow to raise, no mouth to tighten. But Panda has spent two years reading Inumaki off three words and a hand, and Kusakabe can feel himself being read now, the way you'd feel a torch on the back of your neck.

He doesn't look away. That's the other thing he'll remember. He's good at not looking away. He's had years of practice at not looking away from people while not telling them anything.

The radio hisses on Panda's collar. Somewhere under the street, a long way down, something heavy shifts.

"Okay, sensei," Panda says.

He turns back to the stairs.

---

The relief comes first.

It comes fast and it comes warm, all through him, like stepping out of the wind into a shop doorway. His shoulders go down. He didn't know they were up. His hands, which have been cold all night, are suddenly not cold. He breathes all the way to the bottom for the first time since seven o'clock.

*We're not going down.*

*We're staying here. On the stairs. With the small ones.*

It's a good feeling. That's the trouble. It's a really good feeling, and he has it for nearly two seconds.

Then the other thing comes in underneath it. Smaller. Colder. Not a feeling so much as a fact he steps on in the dark, like a stair that isn't there.

*You just lied to a student.*

*To keep yourself off a floor.*

He looks at Panda's back, at the top of the stairs, at the dark patch across one shoulder where something got him in the tunnel. Panda is looking down into the stairwell. Not at anything. Just down.

*Nobody's going to know,* he thinks.

It's true. Tanabe's gone. The radio's gone. Whoever's down there, if anybody is, will never hear about two people on a staircase on Meiji-dori deciding not to come. There'll be no report. No margin. Nobody pencilling a word next to his name.

Nobody's going to know.

He waits for that to make him feel fine.

It doesn't. He doesn't feel fine. He feels small. Not frightened-small. He knows that kind. This is a different size. The size of a man standing at the top of some stairs, telling himself nobody saw.

---

He does the sum anyway. He can't help it. It's what his head does when it's been told not to.

The concourse first. One floor down. Ticket gates, shops with their shutters half down, the long white corridors that all look like each other. Then the next level, and the next. Escalators going down into escalators. Each one a little longer than you'd think. Each landing a place where something could be waiting round the corner, outside the reach of one arm and one sword, until it isn't.

Five of them. Five floors of that, with Panda going first because Panda always goes first, and the air getting thicker the lower you get.

And at the bottom, the platform. A long lit room under the whole city, with the tracks running off into the dark at both ends. And in it, things that make the radio say *grades* with an *s* on the end.

He gets as far as the third escalator in his head and stops there. He doesn't let himself see the platform. He's not sure he could if he tried. He has never stood in a room with a special grade. He's spent his whole working life arranging not to.

*Years,* he thinks. *Years of very careful arranging. And it works. Look. It's still working.*

He looks at the stairs. They go down to the first landing and turn and go out of sight. That's all he can see of them. That's all he needs to.

---

The radio keeps going, in pieces.

"—B five—"

"—can't, it's keyed—"

"—anybody on the—"

He reaches over and turns it down. Not off. Down. Panda looks at the dial and then at him.

"Battery," Kusakabe says.

Panda doesn't say anything. He sits down on the top step, his back to the street, his feet two steps below, and the stair takes his weight with a small complaint from the handrail fixings. He rests his chin on his paws and watches the landing.

Kusakabe stands behind him. Sword out. Circle open, a little. The street at his back. People still coming up now and then, fewer each time, stepping round Panda on the stairs and then stepping round Kusakabe, saying sorry to both of them.

*That's two lies now,* he thinks. *The second one was smaller. That's how they do it. The second one's always smaller.*

---

The curse user comes from the street side, a little after nine.

He feels him before he hears him. Not a curse. A person, with cursed energy coming off him in a hard, steady line, like heat off a road.

A big man. Thirties. A tracksuit with the zip done up to the chin and something wound round his right forearm that might be a chain, and might be something that used to be a chain before somebody did something to it. He's walking down the middle of Meiji-dori in no hurry, through the dropped hats and the cans, and two things are walking with him. Low things. Long. They go on all fours, at his heels, with their heads down.

"Is this the bear?" the man says. He's looking at Panda. "They said there'd be a bear."

Panda stands up.

"Panda," Panda says. "Not a bear. Common mistake."

The man laughs and lets the things go.

The fighting is short. It's always short. It's the waiting that's long.

Panda takes the man. Kusakabe takes what's left. The first of the low things comes in along the ground and meets the line, and his blade is already there, and it isn't any more. The second one doesn't come for him. It goes wide. Round. Along the wall of the stairwell, very fast, towards the place where Panda is standing with his whole back open to it, trading blows with the man in the tracksuit.

Panda's back is outside the circle.

Kusakabe doesn't decide anything. He steps. Two steps, sideways and in, until Panda's back is inside the line. The low thing crosses it. His hands answer. Something hot and thin goes across the back of his knuckles and then the thing is in pieces at Panda's heels, smoking.

Behind him there's a sound like a car door slammed very hard. The man in the tracksuit is sitting against a shop shutter on the other side of the pavement, with the shutter bent round him in a shape it wasn't made in. He isn't getting up. The chain on his arm has gone slack.

Panda looks round at what's smoking by his feet. Then at Kusakabe.

"Thanks."

"Mm."

His knuckles are bleeding a little. Same hand as the tunnel. He seems to be keeping all the damage on the one side tonight, like a man with a system.

*Easy,* he thinks. *Minding a back you can see. Anyone can do that.*

*If we'd gone down, there'd be nobody up here for this.*

He tries that thought on. It almost fits. It's close. It's the right size for the shape of a reason, and if he doesn't move about too much in it, nobody would notice it isn't his.

He takes it off again. He knows where he got it.

---

They tie the man's hands behind him with the cord from somebody's dropped costume. A long red sash from a devil, or a vampire. Panda does the knot. It's a very good knot.

"Where'd you learn that?"

"Masamichi," Panda says. "He ties up all his dolls for the post. Every one, separately. You can't get them open after."

The man in the tracksuit comes round while they're doing it. He looks at Panda, and then at Kusakabe, and then at the stairwell.

"You're going to get it," he says. He sounds pleased about it, the way people are pleased about weather that will ruin someone else's day. "All of you. You've no idea what's down there."

Kusakabe doesn't answer. He doesn't need to. The man's quite right.

Panda looks at him, though. Just for a second. Then he looks away.

---

Afterwards, his knuckles won't stop.

It isn't much. A thin, steady seep across the back of the left hand, running down between the fingers and making the grip slippery. He shakes it. It keeps coming. He wipes it on his trousers. It keeps coming.

Panda holds out a paw. There's a strip of black cloth in it, torn from the bottom of a cape somebody left on the pavement.

"I'm fine."

"You're dripping on the stairs," Panda says. "People have to walk on them."

He holds out his hand.

Panda wraps it. Three times round, and a tuck. His paws are much too big for this and he does it anyway, carefully, with his whole attention, the way he does the knot, the way he does everything. He doesn't pull it too tight. He doesn't make a joke about it. He holds Kusakabe's hand in both paws for a moment after he's finished, the way you'd hold something to check it isn't going to come apart.

Then he lets go.

"There."

"Thanks."

It comes out easily too. Which is strange. Of the two things he's said easily tonight, this is the one that's true, and it's the one that's hard to look at.

---

A small one comes up the stairs at twenty past nine. Barely a curse. Grade 4 at a push. A thing the size of a cat, made mostly of noise, the kind that lives in station toilets and drinks bad moods.

Panda flicks it off the step with the back of a paw, the way you'd flick crumbs off a table, and it bursts on the wall.

"That's five," Panda says.

"I said don't count."

"You said don't. You didn't say why."

---

At some point the radio stops talking about B5F.

Kusakabe doesn't notice it happen. He just notices, sometime after half past nine, that it's been a while. The voices are still there, in pieces. Exits. Users. Someone asking for a medic at a crossing whose name he doesn't catch. Someone counting heads, slowly, and losing their place, and starting again.

But nobody says *B five* any more.

He doesn't know what that means. It could mean it's over down there. It could mean it's over down there.

He doesn't ask.

Panda is sitting on the top step again. He has his head turned a little, the way he does, towards the radio. He's noticed too. Kusakabe can tell from his ears.

"Sensei."

"Yeah."

"The people down there. From ours." Panda doesn't turn round. "Who are they?"

Kusakabe looks at the back of Panda's head. The round ears. The dust in the fur.

"Didn't say," he says.

That's true. Tanabe didn't say. Tanabe didn't say because there was nothing to say. It's the truest thing he's said all night, and it's the worst one, and he can feel the difference between those things like a stone in the hand.

*Building a house out of technically,* he thinks. *Very sound construction. No windows.*

"Okay," Panda says.

---

They don't talk for a while after that.

The street empties. The last group up the stairs is three teenagers who've lost their friend and want to go back down for her, and Panda talks them round, quietly, in the end by promising. Kusakabe doesn't hear what. He only hears Panda's voice, low and even, and then the three of them going up the street, the middle one looking back twice.

Then there's nobody.

Just the stairs, and the light in the stairwell, which is the white, honest light of every station in the city. Advertising boards along the wall going down. A face cream. A language school. A smiling family on a train, going somewhere with mountains.

"Toge's out here somewhere," Panda says, to the empty street. "On another list. And Maki."

"Yeah."

"They'll be all right."

Kusakabe doesn't say *yeah* to that one. He finds he can't. He's run out of the easy ones.

Panda doesn't seem to need it. He nods, once, as if somebody has agreed with him.

Kusakabe sits on the step behind Panda. Higher up. He puts the sword across his knees.

"You scared, sensei?"

Panda doesn't look round when he asks.

Kusakabe thinks about it. He finds he can't be bothered to lie about this one. It would be one too many. There's a limit, apparently. He's found it.

"Yeah."

"Me too."

"You don't look it."

"Neither do you," Panda says. "We're both very good at faces."

Somewhere under them, far down, a long way below the concourse, something gives a single deep knock, like a door being shut in the bottom of a building. Then nothing.

Panda's ears come up. Then, slowly, they go down.

*He knows,* Kusakabe thinks. And then, because he's tired, and the step is cold, and there's no one to see: *He doesn't know anything. He's a student. He believed me.*

He isn't sure which of those he's hoping for.

---

The order comes at ten to ten.

The radio, turned down as it is, still manages it. A man's voice, clear for once, as though he's standing right next to a working aerial.

"All north teams. All north teams. If you're clear, move south. Regroup at the crossing below the station. Repeat. North teams, regroup south."

South. Further in. Not down.

Kusakabe finds he can tell the difference with great precision. He could draw it on a map.

Panda stands up. He does it all at once, the way he does everything, as if gravity is a suggestion he's decided to go along with. He brushes the dust off his knees, and some of it comes off.

"Come on, sensei."

"Yeah."

Kusakabe gets up. His knees tell him about it. He slides the sword home and leaves his hand on the hilt, because his hand wants to be there.

At the top of the stairs he stops, for half a second, and looks down. The landing. The tiles. The arrow on the sign, and the list, and the last name on it.

*Five floors,* he thinks. *That's all. Five floors.*

Then he turns away from it.

Panda has already started down the street.

He walks in the middle of Meiji-dori, on the white lines, where cars are supposed to be. There's nobody to stop him now and nobody to photograph him. He steps over a witch's broom snapped in half. He steps round a child's light-up wand lying in the road, still blinking pink, on and off, for nobody. He doesn't hurry and he doesn't look back, because he doesn't need to. He knows his teacher is behind him.

His back is very broad. The fur is matted across one shoulder, dark and stiff. The radio is clipped at his collar, hissing softly to itself. The round head, the round ears. The plain, steady, rolling walk of someone who believes what he's been told.

Kusakabe looks at it.

He looks at it for about as long as he can. Which isn't long.

Then he looks at the road instead, a step at a time, the white lines going under his feet, and follows.
