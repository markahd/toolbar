# Chapter 16. Under the Rock

He gets his fingers off the hilt in the end.

One at a time. The little finger first, which goes easily. Then the ring finger. The first two take much longer. They've been closed for so long that they've stopped thinking of themselves as a hand, and they open stiffly, with a small ache in every joint, like a gate nobody has used all winter.

He stands there with his hand open at his side and looks at it.

*Still got all of them,* he thinks. *Good. I'll need them for the forms.*

The street is very quiet. Smoke somewhere to the south, low and brown. Nobody running. Nobody shouting. The kind of quiet that isn't peace, just everything holding its breath at once.

He doesn't know where Panda is. He'll go and find out. In a minute.

Then the air changes again.

---

He doesn't fully understand what happens next. Nobody does, at the time.

There are two of them now. He can feel them from here, without trying, the way you can feel two trains in a tunnel through the soles of your feet. One of them is the thing that walked past him. He knows that one. He'll know it for the rest of his life.

The other is new. It's huge, and it isn't a curse he has a grade for, and it isn't anything he has a word for. All he gets of it is a sense of something turning. Slow and heavy and patient, round and round, like a mill wheel in a river that has never once stopped.

They meet somewhere to the south.

*Not near,* he thinks. *Not near enough to matter.*

He's wrong about that.

What he has afterwards is in pieces.

The ground goes first. Not a shake. A drop, as if the whole street has been let down a few centimetres on a rope and caught.

Then the circle. He doesn't draw it. It's just there, all the way out to the edge, and everything is crossing it. Not one thing. Everything. From every side at once, thin and fast and without end, as if the air itself has been filled with edges and set going. His hands try to answer. There's nothing to answer. You can't cut what's already cutting everything.

Across the road, a shop sign comes down in four neat pieces. A lamp post leans, and keeps leaning, in slices. The glass in the windows doesn't shatter. It slides out of its frames in clean sheets, as if it's been told it can go home.

*This isn't for me,* he thinks, with total clarity. *None of this is for me. I'm just standing in it.*

Then fire. A line of it from the south, so bright the street loses all its colours. White, and the black shapes of things in the white. The heat comes a moment after the light, as though it had further to walk.

Then something worse than fire. A pressure, pushing outward from one place, flattening whatever it meets. The building behind him makes a long low sound, almost an animal sound. Concrete splits along lines he can hear.

The world comes down.

---

When he opens his eyes, it's dark.

Not night dark. Night has streetlights in it, and windows, and that faint orange the city puts on the underside of the clouds. This dark has nothing in it at all. He blinks to check his eyes are open. They are. It makes no difference.

He's on his back.

He works that out slowly, from the weight of his own head and the cold under his shoulder blades. Grit under him. Something hard under one hip, not hard enough to matter.

Dust. In his mouth, thick and dry, tasting of chalk. In his eyes, in his nose, in the creases of his neck. The smell of burnt stone, which is not a smell he knew stone could have. He tries breathing through his nose and that's worse. He breathes through his teeth instead, carefully, like a man drinking soup that's too hot.

He lifts his left hand. It goes up about the length of his forearm and stops against something flat.

Cold. Rough. Concrete.

He lays his palm on it and feels along. It goes on further than his arm does. A slab. A big one. It isn't lying on him. It's over him, at an angle, low above his feet and higher above his head, caught at the top on something he can't see. Leaning, the way a sheet of board leans against a wall in a builder's yard.

Under it, there's a pocket of space. Just enough for one man lying down. Not an inch more.

*Somebody measured that,* he thinks. Then: *Nobody measured that.*

On the back of his left hand, under his own fingers, he can feel cloth. The black strip from the cape. Three times round, and a tuck. He finds the tuck with his thumb. Still there. Still holding.

His right hand is still gripping his katana.

He finds that out last. The fingers are closed so hard round the hilt that he'd stopped counting them as his. The blade lies along his side, bare, the tip somewhere down past his knee. He can feel the cold of it through his trouser leg.

He doesn't remember drawing it. He doesn't remember choosing to hold on. A minute before the world came down he was opening those fingers one at a time. Now here they are, closed again, as if nobody had ever asked them to let go.

He didn't choose this. The body did. The body always does.

---

He lies there.

He could move. He tests a few things, small, without meaning anything by it. Toes: yes. Knees: they bend a little, then meet stone. His left side aches in a deep, ordinary way. The cut on his forearm has opened again, warm and sticky under the sleeve. Nothing that's going to kill him. Not quickly.

He could push on the slab and find out how much of it there is. He doesn't.

He could shout.

He thinks about that one properly. He fills his chest to see whether it would work, and the dust comes in with the air, and he coughs. A short dry bark that goes nowhere. The sound stays in the pocket with him. It's very small.

He doesn't try again.

There's a phone in his inside pocket. It has a light on it. He knows exactly where it is. He could reach it with his left hand if he turned his wrist the right way. He doesn't. He has a fair idea of what he'd see, and seeing it wouldn't change anything except how much he'd have to think about it.

Something presses into his right thigh through his trouser pocket. Hard edges on a ring.

Keys. Only two that matter. His own front door. And a ground-floor door twenty minutes away by train, where he always rings the bell first anyway.

---

After a while, because there's nothing else to do with it, he lets the circle out.

Not far. It can't go far. It goes out the reach of one arm and one sword and meets stone everywhere. Stone above, stone below, stone on both sides, close enough to touch. The whole of his circle, every bit of it, full of rock that isn't going anywhere.

Nothing crosses the line. Nothing can. There's no room for anything to come in.

He lies there and feels it for a long moment. It's the safest his circle has ever been.

*Look at that,* he thinks. *Finally got it how I like it.*

He lets it go again.

---

*So this is it,* he thinks.

Not dramatically. He's not built for dramatically. Quietly, the way he'd read a line off a form.

*Grade 1. No technique. Lied to a student to avoid a basement. Stood still while the King of Curses walked past. Got buried under a building I didn't even get to fight in.*

He goes through it again, in case he's left anything out. He hasn't. It's quite a short list.

*What was any of it for?*

He doesn't mean it as a big question. He means it the way you'd mean it about a drawer full of old receipts. All that keeping. For what.

---

He thinks about his sister.

He sees her at the window, because that's where she is. Always. In the high-backed chair with the blue cover she chose and has since forgotten choosing.

It's Wednesday. Halloween. She won't know that. She'll be asleep by now, or awake and not minding which.

Tomorrow's Thursday. The physio comes on Thursdays. That'll happen whether he's under a building or not. He's already paid for it.

Sunday's in four days. Three, if it's past midnight. He doesn't know if it's past midnight.

On Sunday he'll ring the bell and then use his own key. Or he won't.

And if he doesn't, who tells her?

He goes looking for the answer, and there isn't one.

Nobody at school knows she exists. He made very sure of that. Not Ijichi. Not Okabe, with all his files. Not Gojo, who has been through everybody's desk. He told one man, once, at three in the morning, in a break room on the fourteenth floor of an empty building. That's the whole list. One man.

So it would be Ono who noticed. Ono notices everything and mentions almost none of it. She'd ring him, and he wouldn't answer. She'd ring again. After the third time she'd write something down in the notebook with the rabbit on it, and then she'd ring the agency. And Hasegawa would sit at his desk above the dry cleaner's, with the steam coming up the stairwell, and try to work out who you call about a man nobody can tell you anything about.

And his sister would sit at the window on Sunday and look at the door.

Or she wouldn't.

Some Sundays she calls him by their father's name. Some Sundays it takes her until half past four. Maybe she wouldn't know who was missing. Maybe all she'd know is that the tea was different. That somebody else had made it, and made it properly, the way she doesn't like it, and she'd frown at the cup and not know why.

*That's the one,* he thinks. *That's the one I can't do anything with.*

He lies there with it. It doesn't get any smaller.

---

He thinks about Takeru on her lap.

He doesn't think it, exactly. He sees it. The boy sitting the way small children sit once they've decided a lap is theirs, with all of their weight and no apology. And her right hand, the one that can still lift a little, lying flat on his back. Not holding. Just there. Resting on the fact of him.

He has never once said out loud what that was worth. He's never had a number for it. He's tried, sometimes, on trains. There isn't one.

---

He thinks about Yaga.

The debt is where it always is. He checks it, out of habit, here in the dark. He always checks it. He's never once found it gone.

It's there. Of course it's there. It's the only thing in here that hasn't moved.

*Still unpaid.*

Yaga has never mentioned it. Not once, in all these years. Not in a corridor, not in his office with a half-sewn doll on the desk, not when he handed Kusakabe a homeroom. Kusakabe used to think that was tact. Lying here, he isn't so sure. Maybe Yaga just knows what Kusakabe knows. That there's no amount. That a thing like that doesn't get paid back. It only gets carried, for as long as you can carry it, and you hope that counts for something.

If he stays down here, it doesn't get paid. It doesn't get cancelled either. It just stops. Halfway across the room, still in his arms.

*He'd hate this,* Kusakabe thinks. *Losing a perfectly good debtor to a building.*

---

He thinks about his flat.

The stain in the corner above the wardrobe. Two years. Mr. Sato's email address, which he has never once typed.

Somebody will have to clear the flat. That's how it works. Somebody always does. A man from the school, probably, with flattened boxes under his arm and a list. He'll let himself in with a spare key from some office. He'll see the jar of instant coffee by the kettle, and the bag with the sword cleaning kit in it, and the sofa with the shape of one person worn into the left end. He'll look up, the way people do in a stranger's room, and see the stain. And he'll think, *nobody ever reported that,* and he'll be right.

The sofa.

He thinks about the sofa for longer than he means to.

The left end. The cushion gone flat from one particular weight. All the days it's had off him. Not the nights. The nights were fine; everybody sits down at night. The days. Whole days, sat down in at ten in the morning and got up from in the dark. The five in a row last November. The Saturdays. The bank holidays. The afternoons after jobs, when he'd come in, and sit, and that was it, that was the afternoon.

He used to call it rest.

It wasn't rest. He knows that, lying here. They were just days he didn't want to put anything in, in case whatever he put in them asked for something back. So he left them empty. And they went anyway. One after another, quite happily, without him. Nothing in them to hold them down.

He tries to think what he'd have done with them instead. What he'd have put there.

He can't think of anything.

That's the worst part. Not that he wasted them. That he still can't think what they were for.

---

*I avoided everything,* he thinks. *And it still found me.*

It isn't self-pity. He checks. It's more like reading the total at the bottom of a bill.

Because he's good at it. He's very good at it. If there were a box for it on the forms, he'd tick it. A fire door propped open with a mop bucket. The long way round behind the shrine. A phone lying face down on the arm of the sofa while it rang. A covered walkway on a dry morning, taken round the outside, in the rain. A courtyard, and a boy laughing, and the long way round again.

Every one of them felt like a decision. That was the thing. Every one felt like a hand on the wheel. Like he was the one who decided where things went, and what got in, and what didn't.

And tonight, the best one of all. The cleanest work he's ever done. A staircase, a sign with an arrow on it, five floors down. One sentence, in his ordinary voice. *I heard they've already got people down there.* And it worked. It worked perfectly. He kept them both off that floor.

He went south instead. Further in. Not down.

And the floors came down to him.

He nearly laughs. The dust stops him. It comes out as a breath through his nose, and that puts more dust in.

*It felt like steering,* he thinks. *All of it. It was never steering. The world doesn't care which floor you didn't go to. It comes down where it comes down.*

*And you end up here. On your back, in the dark, holding a sword you never got to use. Wondering what you saved yourself for.*

---

He waits to feel frightened.

It would make sense. A man under a building ought to be frightened. He knows exactly what his fear feels like. He's had it all night. Cold palms. The heart going. The circle pulled in tight.

He waits for any of it.

It doesn't come. Fear needs something to spend, and he's got nothing left. He's been spending since seven o'clock.

What's left isn't fear. He turns it over in the dark for a while, trying to find the right word for it, the way he'd look for the right word on a report and then write something shorter instead.

*Lost,* he thinks. *That's the one.*

Not lost like a man in a strange city with the map the wrong way up. Lost like something that's fallen down behind the back of a heavy piece of furniture. Still there. Still exactly itself. Just somewhere nobody's going to think to look.

---

Time goes. He doesn't know how much.

Sounds come through the stone, far off and changed by it, soft at the edges, the way sounds are when you're under the water in a bath. A long rumble that might be another building going. Something that might be screaming, a long way away, that stops. A crack, very sharp. Then another. Then nothing, for a long time.

Then voices.

Two of them, or three. Somewhere up above and to the left. People moving over rubble, loose stuff sliding under their feet. A shout. A name, but not his. He can hear the shape of the words but not the words themselves. They get closer.

He lies still and listens to them, and holds the sword, and doesn't make a sound.

They get quieter. Then they're gone.

*You could have,* he thinks.

*Yeah.*

He tells himself he doesn't know why he didn't. That isn't true. He knows exactly. If he'd called out, they'd have come, and dug, and found him. And then they'd have asked. Are you hurt. Where. Who were you with. Where were you when it happened. Why were you there. And he'd have had to lie there in the light of their torches, with dust in his mouth, and answer.

He'd rather have the dark.

That's not a good thing to know about yourself. He knows it anyway. He's had a lot of practice knowing things about himself and then leaving them where they are.

Dust settles on his face, very lightly, one grain at a time. He doesn't wipe it off.

He closes his eyes. It makes no difference to the dark. But it's a different dark. His own.

*Maybe if I lie here long enough,* he thinks, *someone else will have dealt with it.*

All of it. Whatever turned. Whatever burned. Whatever's still down at the bottom of those five floors. The radios and the stretchers and the lists of names. Somebody will deal with it. Somebody always does. Somebody stronger, or braver, or just better at being found.

And then he'll come out, afterwards. When it's done.

Or he won't. From in here, it's hard to feel much difference.

---

Then the slab moves.

A grinding sound, right over his face. Stone on stone, long and low. A fine trickle of grit across his cheek and into his ear.

He opens his eyes.

Light.

A thin line of it at the high end, above his head. Grey and hard and so bright after the dark that it hurts, and he squints. The line gets wider. Dust pours through it in a sheet, lit all the way down, like something being sieved. The concrete over him shifts. Tilts. And lifts.

He looks up through the dust into a grey sky.

Panda is standing over him.

He's holding the slab above his head with both arms. It's a big slab. Much bigger than it felt from underneath. Panda's legs are set wide on the rubble and his arms are straight and shaking very slightly with the weight. His fur is grey all over with dust and dark in patches with blood, so much of both that he looks like a statue of himself somebody left out in the weather. One ear is torn at the edge. Whatever was on his shoulder in the tunnel is lost under everything that's happened since.

"Sensei," Panda says. "You alive?"

For a moment, Kusakabe can't answer.

He just looks up at him.

The student he lied to. Three hours ago, or four, at the top of a staircase, in his ordinary voice, so that neither of them would have to go down. The student who said *Okay, sensei,* and walked ahead of him down the middle of the street, believing it.

A cursed corpse. Made by the same man, with the same patient hands, as the boy on his sister's lap. The same forbidden work. The same impossible kindness. Standing in the ruins of Shibuya with its arms over its head, holding a building off him.

Panda, who didn't have to come looking.

Who, by the look of him, has been looking for some time.

"Yeah," Kusakabe says. His voice is rough with dust. It sounds like it belongs to somebody older. "Yeah. I'm alive."

"Good," Panda says. "Hang on."

He shifts his feet. Then he turns at the waist and tosses the slab aside like it's a sofa cushion. It goes over with a crash and a great slow cloud, and slides a little way down the heap, and stops. A grey slab of something that used to be a wall, lying on its back, looking at the sky.

Then Panda holds out a paw.

Kusakabe looks at it.

It's grey with dust, like the rest of him. There's blood dried into the pad, dark in the creases. It isn't shaking any more. It's just there. Held out at the height of a man lying on his back. Waiting, with no particular hurry, to see what he'll do. The way it has waited before.

He takes it with his left hand. The bandaged one. Panda's work, three times round and a tuck, still holding.

And he lets himself be pulled up.

---

Standing is worse than lying down.

His knees complain. Nothing under his feet is level. Dust slides off his shoulders and out of his hair, and he coughs, and spits grey, and stands there swaying a little, the sword still in his right hand, and looks.

There's no street.

There's nothing he'd call a street. Where the buildings were there's a slope of broken things going away in every direction, grey on grey, with steel rods coming out of it at angles, like the grass that grows on waste ground. Nothing standing above two floors, as far as the dust lets him see. Small fires here and there, quiet, minding their own business. A traffic light lying on its side, still trying to change.

The shapes the city had are gone. All of them. He doesn't know which way the station is.

He looks at Panda.

Panda is looking at him, head tilted a little, the way he does. Waiting.

"How'd you find me?" Kusakabe says.

Panda thinks about it.

"Kept lifting things," he says.

Kusakabe looks past him, back the way he must have come. The rubble is turned over in patches, a long untidy line of them, slabs pulled up and dropped aside, going back further into the grey than he can see.

He looks at Panda again.

He almost says something.

*Sorry about B5F.* It's right there. Three words. It would be easy, almost.

*Thank you.* Two.

*I didn't deserve that.* Four. The truest, and the worst.

He opens his mouth. There's dust in it. He closes it again.

He doesn't say any of them. They'd come out wrong, or they'd come out right, and he doesn't know which of those he'd be less able to stand there and hear. That's not how he works. He's never been able to make it work that way, and standing in the wreck of Shibuya with grit in his teeth is not the place he's going to learn.

So he says the only thing he's got.

"Let's go."

Panda looks at him for a moment longer. Whatever he sees, he keeps it.

"Okay, sensei."

---

They walk.

There's no road, so they make one. Panda picks the way, over the flat slabs and round the ones that move, testing each step with one foot before he gives it his weight. Kusakabe follows.

Not the way he followed down Meiji-dori, a few hours and a whole city ago, with his eyes on the white lines. Closer than that. Half a step behind, at Panda's left shoulder. Near enough to put out a hand. He doesn't. But he could.

The sword is still in his right hand. He hasn't put it away. He isn't sure he can yet, and he doesn't try.

He looks at Panda's back.

It's grey now, all of it, the same grey as everything. Grey fur, grey dust, the round grey shapes of the ears. He looks at it for a while. Longer than before. Not as long as he'd like.

Once, at the top of a heap of broken concrete, Panda stops and looks back over his shoulder. Not at the way they've come. At him. Just a glance, the kind you'd give a door you've already locked.

Kusakabe is there.

Panda turns round again and goes on down the other side.

Around them, nothing moves but the smoke.

Two shapes in all that grey. One big, one not. Walking carefully, because there's nothing else to do, over what's left of the place.

The smaller one half a step behind. Keeping up.
