# Chapter 2. Sunday: summary

The next Sunday, Kusakabe takes the train, twenty minutes and six stops, to his sister's ground-floor flat with groceries and two persimmons. Mrs. Fujita hands over from her cat-covered notebook, where she writes everything "in case". He takes the pile of bills without comment; he always pays them on Monday at a convenience store and throws away the receipts. Leaving, Mrs. Fujita says the agency's rates are going up fifteen percent next month. He cuts her explanation short with "Fine," without doing the sum. His sister calls him by their father's name and he answers to it. He makes the tea too weak, trims her nails and gives her medicine. While she sleeps he remembers Takeru's death, her breaking, taking her on, and Yaga's gift: the boy on her lap. At half past four she knows him: "Atsuya." On the train home he looks at his reflection over the city and doesn't look away.

## Continuity updates

**New names and facts**
- Sister's flat: ground floor (because of the chair), four-storey building in tired cream tile, street with a laundrette and a dentist; window looks onto a car park and other people's balconies with futons. Six stops, twenty minutes by train.
- Lavender comes from a cheap reed diffuser on the shoe cabinet by the door; he replaces it every two months. "First thing in, last thing out."
- He rings the bell, then uses his own key.
- Window chair: a high-backed armchair with wooden arms from a recycling shop, re-covered in a blue she chose and has since forgotten. A folded wheelchair waits by the wall. Transfers by a "practised hug".
- Mrs. Fujita: fifties, short, square, reading glasses pushed into grey hair, strong forearms, talks fast. About three years with the sister. Four weekday mornings, plus Sunday mornings until he arrives. Has never asked what he does for a job. Keeps a 100-yen notebook with a cartoon cat on it, neat pencil handwriting that slopes uphill; she writes everything "in case". Doesn't like handling money; the agency invoices him. Rate up fifteen percent from next month (weekday hours and Sunday). She said "See you Tuesday."
- An unnamed young night carer from the agency comes at seven on Sundays and says almost nothing.
- The sister can lift a little with her right hand. A spouted plastic cup lid exists. Her daily pills: three white, one yellow ("tastes of metal"), one half pill (dose cut in the summer).
- The sister's repeated memory: a summer at the sea, a guest house with a red roof, a woman who gave them watermelon, sand that burned their feet. New this week: the woman had a dog.
- An upstairs neighbour has a new dog that barks at four.
- He pays the bills every Monday at 7 a.m. at a convenience store near the school and throws the receipts away.
- Yaga came to his kitchen, then to the sister's flat, and was clear it was "not the boy back". Yaga has never once mentioned the debt.
- Nobody at school knows about Sundays: not Ijichi, Okabe or Gojo.
