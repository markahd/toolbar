# Chapter 9. The Clan Problem: summary

June 2018, rainy season. Okabe announces the Zen'in family's annual review of Maki by Zen'in Masaru, a branch-house man. Panda explains Masaru comes yearly to keep Maki at Grade 4; the family writes letters. Maki tells Kusakabe, "Don't do anything." He recognises the clan look and plans to stay out. On Thursday Masaru sits above him on a stool, mocks Maki's glasses and twinhood, praises Mai's modesty in Kyoto, and asks him to amend her eligibility form to Grade 4 work only. Kusakabe says, "I'll look at it." Then Masaru suggests nobody would mind if she had an accident on a mission. Kusakabe stands: "If anything happens to her, I'll come and ask you about it. Personally." Masaru leaves early. Yaga delivers a formal twelve-month reprimand and a deferred allowance review; Kusakabe refuses to apologise. Maki never mentions it, but starts leaving a practice sword on his step. They spar daily. Closing: she tosses it to him. "Again." "Fine."

## Continuity updates

**New names and facts**
- Zen'in Masaru: late fifties, short, well-fed, dark grey haori with small white family crest, hakama, white tabi (muddied, offended). Soft voice that is never raised. Calls Kusakabe "Kusakabe-kun". Comes every year (Panda: "He brings his own tea. In a flask."). Drove from Kyoto. Sits on a folding wooden stool at the top of the steps. A young unnamed attendant holds a black umbrella over him, even under the roof; the attendant's right shoulder is soaked.
- Masaru's lines: "How suitable. They've given her someone she can look up to without straining her neck."; "A Zen'in who has to put something on her face to see what she's for."; twins unlucky, "perhaps two halves would make something. They didn't."; Mai "has the sense to be modest about it. In Kyoto. She knows her place."; "We look at her once a year. That's quite enough."; "Men like you usually are [practical]. You haven't the luxury of being otherwise."; the accident line ("It would close a file that's been open for sixteen years... You're not paid enough to try very hard.").
- Maki is Grade 4 because "the family writes letters" (Panda: "She'll be Grade 4 when she's ninety."). Kusakabe's monthly eligibility form had her "Eligible for Grade 2 accompaniment"; Masaru asked him to amend it to Grade 4 only; he said "I'll look at it" and did not (left unresolved on the page).
- Maki to Kusakabe beforehand: "Thursday. Don't do anything." / "Wasn't going to." / "Good."
- Kusakabe's line: "If anything happens to her, I'll come and ask you about it. Personally." He didn't touch his sword or draw the circle. He sat back down without answering "Was that a threat?" Masaru left twenty minutes early. Panda heard everything; Maki probably didn't (left ambiguous). Inumaki reached for his collar zip and Kusakabe stopped him with just his name.
- Consequences: Zen'in letter (cream paper, grey crest) says he "openly threatened a representative of the family in front of students." Formal reprimand on his record for twelve months; grade allowance review (was due August) deferred a year. He did the sum this time; it hurts (Ono's hours, thickening powder, the diffuser). The family wants a written apology; he said "No"; Yaga will tell them he's "drafting it. Drafts take time." Yaga: "Don't make a habit of it." / "Won't need to." Okabe brought the acknowledgement in a clear plastic sleeve: "That's two this term" (with Sakaguchi's May complaint), "There's a meeting. In September." Okabe started "For what it's worth" and didn't finish.
- Sparring: Maki leaves a taped practice sword on his third step every afternoon. He spars WITHOUT the circle (only feet, distance, eyes). Ten minutes, rising to twenty. His one correction: "You look at where you're going to hit." (fixed in four days). Bruises up both forearms; his sister asked if he'd been fighting and seemed pleased.
- Kusakabe's age given vaguely as "thirty-something".
